Milo

A calm helper for the web,
for people who find it hard.

Watch the demo See it on GitHub

What Milo does for you

  • Stops risky payments

    A pause before every payment so you can check who is paid and how much.

  • Labels fake buttons

    Marks adverts that look like buttons so you do not click the wrong thing.

  • Warns about scams

    Slows down when a page asks for gift-card codes or wire transfers.

  • Steadies shaky clicks

    Helps when a hand tremor means you click the wrong place.

Who it is for

Milo is for older adults and for people with hand tremor. It is for anyone who gets confused by pages that move, or is not sure which button is safe to press. You do not need to know anything about computers to use it.

Why it matters

The web was not designed for older adults or for people whose hands shake. These numbers show the gap.

55.3%

task completion rate for adults 65 and older on websites, against 74.5% for adults aged 21–55.

Nielsen Norman Group, 2013
10M

Americans live with essential tremor — the most common movement disorder, and one that makes clicking harder.

NIH · NINDS
77%

of adults 65 and older say they would need someone to walk them through using a new device or app.

Pew Research Center, 2014

Why the web is hard

  • Pages move and pop up. Adverts that look like real buttons.
  • A shaky hand can click the wrong thing.
  • Scams ask for gift-card codes.

What Milo does

Ask it anything

Type or speak your question. Milo answers in plain words and points at the button on the page.

Fill in forms

Fills in forms with your saved details, after asking you first.

Pay Pause

Stops before any payment and tells you who is paid, how much, and if it repeats.

Scam warning

A longer pause when a page asks for gift-card codes, wire transfers or remote access.

Fake-button check

Marks adverts that pretend to be buttons.

"I'm stuck"

Sends one short fixed email to someone you trust, like a family member.

Milo also steadies shaky clicks, and warns when a shop asks for an ID number.

How it works

  1. 1

    Milo reads the page

    It looks at the words and buttons on the page you are visiting.

  2. 2

    You ask a question

    Type or speak. No special words needed.

  3. 3

    Milo answers in plain words

    It points at the button you need and tells you what it does.

Honest limits

Milo only checks and reminds. It does not block anything and cannot promise you are safe. It never presses pay, send or submit for you, and never fills passwords, card numbers or codes.

Your privacy

Your saved details and your list of sites stay on your device. The only thing Milo sends out by itself is one short "I'm stuck" email, and only when you press the button. It contains two first names, the task name and the step number, nothing else.

When you ask a question, it goes to Google Gemini through our relay. We send the names of the buttons and the words you can read on the page, so Milo can answer questions about it. We never send what you type. Pages for signing in or paying, and pages that look like your bank or your health records, are not read at all. You can switch reading off in Settings.

Most of the time Milo works from the words and buttons on the page. If you ask Milo to look at the page and it cannot tell from the words alone, it can send a picture of what is on your screen to the helper that answers you. It only does this when you ask, and it tells you when it has looked at a picture.

When you talk to Milo, Chrome turns your voice into words: on your computer when it can, otherwise with Google's speech service.
